# Where Quote Requests Die: The Intake Path Audit Nobody Runs

A Metro Detroit business spends real money replacing a landing page with a proper website. Traffic arrives. People fill out the form. The phone rings a little more than it used to. And the calendar barely moves.

The owner concludes the website failed. Usually the website did its job. It delivered the request. Somewhere between the inbox and the calendar, the business lost it — and nobody in the building owns that stretch of road. Traffic has an owner. Closing has an owner. The middle has nobody.

## Seven Handoffs, One Number

A quote request does not travel. It is handed off, seven times:

1. Form start → submit
2. Submit → acknowledgement
3. Ring → answered
4. Answered → the person who can quote
5. Conversation → quote sent
6. Quote → follow-up
7. Decision → outcome recorded

Every handoff has a failure rate, and none has to be catastrophic to be expensive. Seven handoffs each completing 90% of the time leave you 48% end to end. At 80% each, 21%. At 70% each, 8%. No stage looks broken in that scenario, and the chain still destroys most of your demand.

That is why "the website isn't working" is usually the wrong diagnosis. Page structure is covered in [website architecture](/blog/2026-10-08-one-page-cant-do-five-jobs-website-architecture), conversion basics in [the fundamentals](/blog/2026-08-31-make-your-website-convert). This is the part after the form works.

## Handoff One: They Start the Form and Stop

Zuko's 2025 benchmarking across 93 million form sessions found that 34% of people who start a form never complete it.

In Metro Detroit, a service form often asks for a budget range, a timeline, a service category, a preferred contact method, and a message — eight fields for a job that starts with a phone call. Three is the working target: name, phone, one line about the job. Every extra required field taxes a person who has not decided to trust you yet.

## Handoff Two: Silence After Submit

The submission lands in an inbox that one person checks between jobs.

Invoca's May 2026 survey of 693 U.S. consumers who had made a high-stakes purchase found 79% will switch to a competitor that responds faster, and 27% contact another brand when a reply runs late. A confirmation email is not a courtesy; it is the difference between a request in a queue and one that still belongs to you.

Sixty seconds of automation — "we have your request, here is what happens next" — closes this handoff completely.

## Handoff Three: The Phone Nobody Answers

This is where the data gets interesting, and where most published numbers fall apart.

Invoca's 2026 Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report, drawn from more than 70 million calls across 10 industries, puts the share of calls answered by a person at 56% — rising to 65% for calls longer than 15 seconds, which filters out misdials. These are Invoca's own customers, not a national sample. CallRail's 2026 platform data puts unanswered business calls at 28%, without disclosing how many calls the average covers.

The number you have probably seen — 62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered — traces to a January 2016 study of 85 businesses over 30 days, and it counts voicemail as unanswered. Real research, narrow sample, repeated for a decade with the year usually wrong.

The consequences are better measured than the rates. In CallRail's 2025 survey of 1,000 U.S. consumers, 78% had taken their business elsewhere after failing to reach a company by phone, 82% would call a competitor rather than wait, and only 42% leave a voicemail.

## Handoff Four: Hold Music Is Still a Handoff

Answering is not the same as answering usefully.

In the same CallRail survey, 41% hang up after one to two minutes on hold and only 18% will wait more than five; Invoca's 2026 report found 75% had hung up over hold time, up from 50% a year earlier. A shop in Oakland County closes at five; the receptionist picks up, the caller asks for a quote, and the transfer becomes an eighth handoff nobody counted.

## Handoff Five: The Quote That Takes a Week

The most-quoted research in this field is the 2007 study by InsideSales.com and Dr. James Oldroyd, then at MIT: three years, six companies, more than 15,000 web leads, 100,000 call attempts. Leads called within five minutes were 100 times more likely to be contacted and 21 times more likely to qualify than leads called at 30 minutes.

Its limits matter and are rarely stated: 2007, a conference summary rather than a peer-reviewed paper, six companies on one calling system, vendor co-authored. It measured contact and qualification — not closed deals.

What survives those limits is the survey in the same paper: 495 companies, where each step slower in time-to-first-call corresponded to 4.3% fewer qualified leads. In that same survey, 38% of companies did not know, did not track, or would not say how long their first call took.

Mystery shops show the practice. In a 2024 shop of 92 franchise brands, half followed up on an online form at all, and only 56% of those called within eight hours. In a 2016 UK shop, 35% of law firms that received a website enquiry had not answered it two to three days later.

[The cutover](/blog/2026-10-26-the-cutover-replace-landing-page) is where most businesses stop. The quote is where the money actually leaks.

## Handoff Six: Two Attempts and Done

InsideSales research puts the average number of call attempts on a new lead at 1.3, while six attempts reaches up to 90% of leads. It is vendor research and should be read as directional, but the direction is not controversial: most requests are abandoned by the business, not by the buyer.

The local version is unglamorous. The second call. The text two days later. The email that says "still interested, or should I close the file?" Those three touches are the difference between a quote and a job, and they take eleven minutes.

## Handoff Seven: Nobody Records the Outcome

If your quote log has no outcome column, the leak is permanent, because it is invisible. Every stage above becomes anecdote instead of measurement.

This is also where the record matters. A business that cannot prove when a request arrived and when it responded cannot audit its own intake path — and in regulated work, cannot defend the file either. The record is the audit.

## The 90-Minute Audit

Four exports you already own:

| Export | What it proves | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Form submissions with timestamps | How many requests actually arrived | Form plugin, host, or CRM |
| Call log with answered / missed / duration | Whether the phone is a handoff or a hole | Phone system or carrier portal |
| Quote log with sent dates | Whether a request became a quote, and how fast | Invoices, spreadsheet, or CRM |
| Calendar or job log with source | Which requests became work | Scheduler or accounting system |

Pull 90 days. Compute the conversion rate at each of the seven handoffs. Rank the drops. Fix the largest one for 30 days and re-measure.

A business that fixes its form but not its phone has optimized the stage that was already working.

## Six Fixes, Ranked by Effort

| Fix | Handoff it closes | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic acknowledgement within 60 seconds | 2 | One rule in the form tool |
| After-hours text-back or answering | 3 | A phone setting or a service |
| Trim the form to three fields | 1 | An afternoon |
| A 24-business-hour quote standard | 5 | A decision, then a calendar block |
| Six-attempt follow-up cadence | 6 | Templates plus a reminder |
| An outcome field on every quote | 7 | One column |

None of these require new staff, a new website, or a new budget line.

## Where the Stack Earns Its Keep

Two points in the intake path genuinely need more than a spreadsheet.

The first is a multi-party handoff — you, a subcontractor, an insurer, a client, each with a different copy of what was agreed and when. A [Chainlink Runtime Environment](/blog/2026-10-29-automation-stops-at-company-line) workflow timestamps each step and makes the record tamper-evident, so the intake path produces verifiable workflows instead of three inboxes and an argument. That is the honest use of CRE: not crypto for its own sake, but a record no party can quietly rewrite.

The second is drafting. A Local LLM running on-premises can summarize an inbound request and draft the acknowledgement, the clarifying questions, the first pass at the quote — without the inquiry leaving the building. For a firm holding privileged material or a practice working under HIPAA, private AI that stays on-premises is the only version of this automation that does not create a new liability while solving an old one.

The website is the front door. A professional web presence is worth exactly what the path behind it is worth — and if that path is instrumented, the site stops being a brochure and becomes the first stage of a system you can measure. That is what separates a future-proof business from one that keeps buying new front doors.

## Start With the Leak You Can See

Pick one export, one week, one stage. The phone log is usually the fastest start, because the gap between "rang" and "answered" is a number, not an opinion.

Fix the biggest drop and leave everything else alone for 30 days. One handoff at a time is how you tell a failing website from a business dropping what the website delivers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a lead intake audit?

It is a stage-by-stage measurement of what happens to a quote request between submission and a recorded outcome — form completion, acknowledgement, answer rate, time to first response, quote turnaround, follow-up attempts, and whether the outcome was logged. It measures the business, not the website.

### What percentage of business calls go unanswered?

CallRail's 2026 platform data puts it at 28%, and Invoca's 2026 benchmark finds 56% of calls reach a person at all, rising to 65% for calls over 15 seconds. The widely repeated 62% figure comes from a 2016 study of 85 businesses that counted voicemail as unanswered.

### How fast should a business respond to a website inquiry?

Automated acknowledgement should be under a minute, and a human response should come within one business hour. The 2007 MIT/InsideSales study found leads called within five minutes were 21 times more likely to qualify, though it measured contact and qualification from six companies — direction, not a guarantee.

### How many times should you follow up on a quote?

Six attempts across call, text, and email. InsideSales research puts the average at 1.3 attempts, which means most quotes are abandoned by the seller, not rejected by the buyer.

### What is a normal form abandonment rate?

Zuko's 2025 benchmark of 93 million form sessions found 34% of people who start a form never complete it. Anything materially above that points at field count, mobile layout, or the lack of an obvious next step.
