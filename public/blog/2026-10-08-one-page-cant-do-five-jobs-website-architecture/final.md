# One Page Can't Do Five Jobs: The Website Architecture That Gets Metro Detroit Businesses Found

*Published: October 8, 2026 | By Agentic*

---

A landing page is a fine place to start. It is a terrible place to stay.

When an established business keeps everything on a single page, that page is quietly asked to do five jobs at once: rank for every service, speak to every kind of buyer, prove credibility, answer objections, and convert. No page does all five well. Search engines match queries to pages, not to businesses — and one page answers one cluster of searches at a time.

The fix is not more copy. It is more structure: one page, one job.

## Google Ranks Pages, Not Businesses

Here is the mechanism most owners never hear. When someone searches "emergency drain cleaning Oakland County," Google is not looking for the best plumbing business. It is looking for the best page about emergency drain cleaning in Oakland County. A business with one combined services page competes for that query with a paragraph. A business with a dedicated page competes with an entire page written for it.

Dedicated service pages are repeatedly identified as a top on-page factor in local organic rankings (Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors). The reason is structural. A page that talks about ten services competes for all ten queries and wins almost none, because it is diluted across everything it mentions. Ten specific pages compete independently, each with a real chance at one search.

That is why the single-page site loses a race it never sees coming. It enters one horse into fifty heats.

## The Same Decision Fixes Conversion

Ranking is only half of it. A dedicated page also closes better. In 2026 data, a dedicated landing page converts at a **median 4.02%**, against **2.35%** for a general page — and the top quarter of pages clear **11.45%**. When a visitor lands on a page that names exactly their problem, they can tell in a second that they are in the right place.

## The Architecture: One Page, One Job

Most local businesses need the same skeleton. Each page carries exactly one responsibility, and none of them can be merged away without losing something.

| Page | The one job it does | Why it can't be merged |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Orient a stranger in five seconds — what you do, where, and what to do next | It is the only page every visitor sees; it has to stay broad |
| **Services hub** | Show the full range and route visitors to the right detail | It links down to service pages; without it they are hard to reach |
| **One page per service** | Rank for that service's searches and convert that specific buyer | A combined page dilutes across every service it lists |
| **Service-area pages (2–3 core)** | Prove you serve a specific town, with real local detail | Generic pages cannot match "near me" intent |
| **About / proof** | Carry the trust signals — people, credentials, completed work | Buyers check it; it is consistently a top-three visited page |
| **Contact** | Remove every reason not to reach you | Around 44% of buyers leave a site that hides contact information |

Everything else is optional until there is a reason for it. That is the discipline: a page exists because it has a job, not because a template came with a slot.

## How Many Pages Is That?

For most local businesses, the answer lands between five and twenty pages — most starting with five to ten focused ones (Scalify, 2026) and building toward the eight-to-twenty range where local service sites do best. The number is a result, not a target, and a simple rule produces it:

**Count your real services. Count the towns that drive revenue. Add the essentials — home, about, contact, at least one service page. That total is your site.**

Three services in one town lands near eight pages; six services across four towns, nearer twenty.

## Service-Area Pages, Done Honestly

Location pages are where businesses either win quietly or hurt themselves loudly. Done well — with real local detail, your own proof, and content written for that town — businesses with optimized location pages see about **37% more leads** (BrightLocal, 2026). Done lazily, by swapping the town name onto an otherwise identical page, they become thin content, and a large share of thin pages can drag down the rankings of the whole site.

Build two or three strong area pages before you build twenty. If you cannot write something true and specific about serving a town, that page is not ready to exist.

## More Pages Is Not the Same as More Traffic

This is the trap on the other side, and it undoes more sites than a single page ever does. Search engines evaluate overall site quality, and a pile of near-identical or filler pages can pull down the pages that genuinely deserve to rank. A tight site where every page has a job beats a bloated one full of padding — every time.

## The Wiring Between Pages

Architecture is not only which pages exist. It is how they link. Internal links move authority from your strongest pages to the ones that need it, and they tell search engines how your site fits together. Strong internal linking can lift rankings by up to **40%** and improve crawl efficiency by **40% to 70%**.

Depth matters as much as count. Pages buried four or more clicks from the homepage get roughly **70% less crawl attention** than pages within two clicks, and orphaned pages — those reachable only by typing the URL — waste about **40%** of a site's internal link value.

The practical wiring: home links to the services hub; the hub links to every service page; each service page links back and sideways to its closest relatives; every page links to contact. Keep anything that matters within three clicks of the home page.

## Why This Matters More in the AI Search Era

Structured, multi-page sites are not just better for Google. They are what AI answer engines cite. Google's AI Overviews now appear in roughly **half** of searches, and pages cited inside them earn about **35% more organic clicks** than non-cited competitors.

The citation bar is high. Research on ChatGPT's sourcing found it cites only about **15%** of the pages it retrieves, and pages carrying FAQ schema and inline citations see roughly **40% higher citation weighting**. Nearly **44%** of AI citations come from the first 30% of a page's content.

A single landing page gives an AI almost nothing to extract. A structured site hands it a page for every question a buyer might ask, answered near the top — the difference between being the source an engine quotes and being invisible.

## The Five-Step Page Audit

Before you buy another ad or another tool, count your pages honestly:

1. **List every service you sell.** Does each have its own page? If two share one, split them.
2. **List the towns that drive revenue.** Do two or three have a real, specific page — not a swapped headline?
3. **Open your site on a phone and find your phone number.** If it takes more than a few seconds, the contact path is costing you work.
4. **Click from the home page to your most important service page.** Count the clicks. More than three, and it is buried.
5. **Cut the dead weight.** Any page that answers no buyer question and moves no one toward contact is a liability, not an asset.

## When One Page Is Actually Enough

Not every business needs a ten-page site, and pretending otherwise is how agencies lose trust. If you sell a single service, serve one area, and get most work by referral, one excellent page — clear offer, real proof, obvious contact — can be exactly right. Add pages when there is a service to rank, an area to serve, or a question to answer. Architecture follows the business; it does not lead it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many pages should a small business website have?**
Most small business sites perform best with five to ten focused core pages to start, growing toward seven to fifteen; local service businesses do well in the eight-to-twenty range. The right number comes from counting your services and priority areas, not a template.

**Does every service need its own page?**
If you offer three or more distinct services, yes. A dedicated page per service is a top on-page factor for local rankings, and dedicated pages convert at roughly double the rate of general ones. A single well-built services page is fine if you offer only one or two services.

**When do I need service-area or location pages?**
Only for the towns that drive real revenue, and only when you can write something genuinely specific about each. Businesses with optimized location pages see about 37% more leads, but a page per town built by swapping the place name is thin content that can hurt the whole site.

**Can too many pages hurt my SEO?**
Yes — if the extra pages are thin. Search engines judge overall site quality, and unhelpful pages can pull down your good ones. More pages help only when each is substantive.

**What pages does every local business website need?**
Four to start: a home page, an about page, a contact page, and at least one service page (a hub if you sell several). From that core you add one page per service and per priority area.

**How does site architecture affect AI search like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews?**
AI engines cite structured, multi-page sites far more than single landing pages. ChatGPT cites only about 15% of the pages it retrieves, and FAQ schema plus first-paragraph answers raise citation odds by roughly 40%. A page per question is far more citable than one page covering everything.

**Do I need internal links between my pages?**
Yes. Internal links pass authority to the pages that need it, improve crawl efficiency by 40–70%, and can lift rankings by up to 40%. Keep every important page within three clicks of the home page and eliminate orphan pages.
